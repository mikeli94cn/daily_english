The Nobel Prize is widely regarded as the world's most prestigious intellectual accolade. It was established by the 1895 will of Alfred Nobel, a wealthy Swedish chemist, engineer, and the inventor of dynamite. 

Administered by the Nobel Foundation in Stockholm, the prizes have been awarded annually since 1901 to individuals or organizations that have conferred the "greatest benefit to humankind." 

The Six Prize Categories

The prizes recognize monumental achievements across six distinct fields:
* Physics (awarded by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences)
* Chemistry (awarded by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences)
* Physiology or Medicine (awarded by the Karolinska Institute)
* Literature (awarded by the Swedish Academy)
* Peace (awarded by the Norwegian Nobel Committee chosen by Norway's parliament)
* Economic Sciences (Established separately in 1968 by Sweden's central bank, the Sveriges Riksbank, in memory of Alfred Nobel, but fully integrated into the Nobel proceedings) 

Key Details & Financial Reward

* The Prize Payout: In celebration of the 125th anniversary of the first awards, the Nobel Foundation increased the prize purse to 12 million Swedish kronor (SEK) per full category (roughly US$1.21 million). If a single category has up to three joint winners, they divide the money according to specific committee rulings. 
* The Material Rewards: Alongside the money, every laureate receives a unique, custom-made handmade artwork diploma and a 18-karat gold-plated green gold medal featuring Alfred Nobel's profile. 
* The Ceremonies: By tradition, the winners are announced every year in early October. The formal presentation ceremonies take place annually on December 10th (the anniversary of Nobel's death). The Peace Prize is presented in Oslo, Norway, while the other five are presented by the King of Sweden in Stockholm. 

Core Rules & Selection

Nominations are strictly invitation-only and remain sealed in the Nobel archives for 50 years to ensure privacy. 

Per the official statutes, the awards cannot be given posthumously (the recipient must be alive at the time of the October announcement), and a single category prize can be shared by a maximum of three individuals. Only the Peace Prize can be formally awarded to entire organizations or humanitarian groups.

The Shaw Prize is an international award established in 2002 by the late Hong Kong media mogul and philanthropist Mr. Run Run Shaw. Frequently referred to as the "Nobel of the East," it honors individuals who have achieved significant breakthroughs in scientific research and whose work has benefited humankind. 

Core Prize Categories

Originally awarded across three fields starting in 2004, the Shaw Prize Foundation expanded the categories in 2026 to include a fourth discipline: 
* Astronomy
* Life Science and Medicine
* Mathematical Sciences
* Computer Science (Introduced in January 2026 to celebrate foundational advances transforming modern society) 

Key Details & Financial Reward

* The Prize: Each annual award consists of a gold medal, a certificate, and a monetary prize of US$1.2 million. 
* Medal Design: The front of the gold medal features an etched portrait of Sir Run Run Shaw, while the back features an abstract 'S' within a circle, symbolizing scientific discovery. 
* Overlapping Prestige: The prize is highly regarded within the global scientific community. Over 120 individuals have been recognized, and notably, 16 Shaw Laureates have subsequently gone on to win a Nobel Prize.
  
Process & Selection

The prize operates through a strict, invitation-only nomination process that runs annually from late summer through autumn. Dedicated selection committees—composed of internationally renowned scientists, including past Turing Award winners—adjudicate the nominations to select the next generation of laureates.

 the "Life Science and Medicine" category in the Shaw Prize (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F032v3d&q=Shaw+Prize) largely overlaps and repeats the scope of the Nobel Prize in "Physiology or Medicine." 
 
 Both awards are designed to recognize groundbreaking discoveries that fundamentally change our understanding of biological systems or pave the way for major medical breakthroughs.

However, while their target fields of study are highly similar, they function with distinct operational and geographical differences:

How They Interact (The Laureate Overlap)

Because both committees search for the absolute pinnacle of biological and medical achievements, they frequently honor the exact same scientists for the exact same discoveries.

Historically, winning a Shaw Prize in Life Science and Medicine is a strong indicator that a scientist might eventually win a Nobel Prize. 

Several legendary researchers have won both for identical breakthroughs:
* Robert Lefkowitz & Brian Kobilka: Won the Shaw Prize in 2007 and later the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2012 for their work on G-protein-coupled receptors.
* Shinya Yamanaka: Won the Shaw Prize in 2008 and the Nobel Prize in 2012 for discovering induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells.
* Tu Youyou: Won the Nobel Prize in 2015 for discovering artemisinin to treat malaria, a field heavily recognized across global health prizes like the Shaw Prize.
* Jennifer Doudna & Emmanuelle Charpentier: Won the Shaw Prize in 2016 and the Nobel Prize in 2020 for the development of CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing.
