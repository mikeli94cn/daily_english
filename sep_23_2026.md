Here is a comprehensive breakdown of essential workplace vocabulary organized by the phases of your workday and your days away from the job.
## 🟢 1. Beginning Work (Start of the Day)
These terms and phrases describe arriving at the workplace and officially starting your tasks for the day. [1, 2] 

* 
* Clock in / Punch in: To officially record the time you arrive at work, usually by scanning a badge or using a digital app.
* Start work: A simple phrase used to tell someone what time your day begins (e.g., "I start work at 9:00 AM.").
* Come in: Arriving at your workplace (e.g., "I need to come in early tomorrow.").
* Shift start: The exact scheduled time your work block begins.
* Log on: Used primarily by remote workers to describe turning on their computers and starting their remote work system. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 
* 

## 🟡 2. During Work (On-Duty Time)
These terms describe the hours you spend performing your job duties, including required hours and extra hours. [2, 5] 

* 
* Core time: The specific hours of the day when all employees must be present at work, especially in companies with flexible schedules.
* Flexitime: A system that allows employees to choose their own start and finish times, provided they complete a required number of hours.
* Shift: A set period of time worked by a group of employees (e.g., a morning shift, night shift, or graveyard shift).
* Overtime: Time worked beyond your normal, scheduled hours (e.g., "I worked three hours of overtime this week.").
* Work late / Stay late: Remaining at your workplace past your regular departure time. [2, 6, 7, 8] 
* 

## 🔴 3. Ending Work (Off-Work Time - Daily)
These phrases describe finishing your daily tasks and leaving your physical or remote workplace. [1, 4] 

* 
* Clock out / Punch out: To officially record the time you leave work at the end of your day.
* Get off (work): A common, casual way to say you are finished with your workday (e.g., "I get off work at 5:30 PM.").
* Clock off: Another term for finishing your work shift for the day.
* Be done: Finishing your tasks for the day (e.g., "I am done by 5:00 PM.").
* Head home / Head back: Physically leaving the office or work site to go back to your personal life. [1, 2, 3, 4, 7] 
* 

## 🔵 4. Days Off (Off-Work Time - Extended)
These terms describe periods when you are not expected to work for an entire day, week, or longer. [8, 9] 

* 
* Time off: A general term for any time away from work, whether for a vacation, doctor's appointment, or personal reasons.
* Day off: A single full day where you do not have to work.
* Half-day off: Taking only the morning or the afternoon off from work.
* Annual leave: The total number of paid days off your employer gives you each year for vacations.
* Sick leave: Paid or unpaid time off allowed by your employer when you are ill or injured.
* Staycation: Taking time off to rest and relax at home instead of traveling somewhere else. [2, 6, 8, 9, 10]
