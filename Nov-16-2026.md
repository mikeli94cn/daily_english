Accounting and finance work together to track historical business transactions and plan future capital growth. While accounting focuses on accurate record-keeping and compliance, finance uses that data to manage risk, raise funds, and maximize value.

Core Accounting Topics

* Financial Accounting: Prepares standardized reports (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement) for external stakeholders like investors, lenders, and HMRC.
* Management Accounting: Focuses on internal decision-making through budgeting, cost-volume-profit analysis, and variance reporting.
* Taxation & Compliance: Covers corporate tax, income tax, payroll (PAYE), and digital tax reporting frameworks like UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT.
* Auditing & Assurance: Involves independent verification of financial records to ensure accuracy and prevent fraud under standards like UK GAAP or IFRS.

Core Financial Topics

* Corporate Finance: Manages a company's capital structure, deciding how to fund operations through debt (loans, bonds) or equity (issuing shares).
* Financial Planning & Analysis (FP&A): Uses historical accounting data to build financial models, forecast future cash flows, and evaluate project profitability (using metrics like NPV and IRR).
* Working Capital Management: Optimizes short-term liquidity by balancing cash reserves, inventory turnover, accounts receivable, and accounts payable.
* Investment & Risk Management: Evaluates asset allocation, interest rate exposure, currency fluctuations, and market returns.

Accounting vs. Finance at a Glance


|Attribute|Accounting|Finance|
|---|---|---|
|Primary Focus|	Recording, classifying, and summarizing past transactions|	Managing assets, liabilities, and future cash flows|
|Time Horizon|	Backward-looking (historical accuracy)|	Forward-looking (projections and strategy)|
|Main Audience|	Regulators, tax authorities, auditors, and shareholders|	Executives, investors, and portfolio managers|
|Key Outputs|	Ledgers, tax returns, and statutory financial statements|	Budgets, valuations, risk models, and investment plans|
