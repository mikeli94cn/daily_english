# Mercury(Hg)
In chemistry, Hg is the chemical symbol for Mercury, a transition metal with the atomic number 80. Located in Group 12 and Period 6 of the periodic table, it is uniquely famous as the only metal that remains a liquid at standard room temperature and pressure. [1, 2] 

The symbol Hg comes from its traditional Latin name, Hydrargyrum, which translates to "water-silver" or "liquid silver" due to its fluid, shiny appearance. [1, 3] 

## 📊 Atomic & Physical Profile
Mercury is exceptionally dense—roughly 13.5 times denser than water. Because of this extreme density, solid iron, copper, and even lead bricks will float directly on top of liquid mercury. [3, 4]

## 🔬 Unique Chemical Behaviors


* High Surface Tension: Mercury atoms form strong metallic bonds with each other. When spilled, it does not wet surfaces like water; instead, it pulls inward tightly to form perfectly rounded, highly mobile beads. [2, 3] 
* Amalgamation (Alloy Formation): Mercury readily dissolves other metals—such as gold, silver, tin, and zinc—to create mixtures known as amalgams. [5, 6] 
* Aluminum Corrosion: Because mercury dissolves aluminum and destroys its protective oxide layer, it causes structural aluminum to rapidly corrode. For this reason, liquid mercury is strictly banned on aircraft.
* Natural Occurrence: Mercury is rare in the Earth's crust. It is primarily harvested by roasting a bright red volcanic mineral ore called cinnabar (mercury sulfide, $\text{HgS}$). [2, 7] 
  


## 🛑 Toxicity & Safety Considerations
While liquid mercury itself is poorly absorbed by the digestive tract, it is highly volatile and evaporates at room temperature into an invisible, completely odorless, and highly toxic vapor. [8, 9] 


* The Danger: Inhaling mercury vapor or contacting organic forms (like methylcomposing fish toxins) attacks the central nervous system, brain, and kidneys. It causes severe neurological damage, tremors, and cognitive deficits. [1, 5, 10] 
* Phase-Out: Because it accumulates in the food chain (bioaccumulation), global initiatives like the [Minamata Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minamata_Convention_on_Mercury) have phased out its use in common household items like glass thermometers and barometers. [1, 5]
