# top 10 mathematicians
Historians and mathematicians widely regard a core group of figures as the greatest and most influential mathematicians of all time, based on consensus from historical analysis. 

Top 10 Greatest Mathematicians of All Time

Rank / Figure	Era / Lifetime	Key Contributions
1. Leonhard Euler (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F04jzj&q=Leonhard+Euler)	1707–1783	Pioneered graph theory, Euler's identity (\(e^{i\pi}+1=0\)), complex analysis, and modern mathematical notation.
2. Carl Friedrich Gauss (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01t_z&q=Carl+Friedrich+Gauss)	1777–1855	Known as the "Prince of Mathematicians"; major advances in number theory, differential geometry, and the Gaussian (normal) distribution.
3. Sir Isaac Newton (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F03s9v&q=Sir+Isaac+Newton)	1643–1727	Co-inventor of calculus and creator of the mathematical foundation for classical mechanics in Principia Mathematica.
4. Archimedes (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0tnc&q=Archimedes)	c. 287–212 BC	Calculated an accurate approximation of π, laid down the principles of buoyancy and levers, and advanced early calculus concepts.
5. Bernhard Riemann (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0bjvq&q=Bernhard+Riemann)	1826–1866	Formulated Riemannian geometry and the Riemann hypothesis, paving the way for Einstein's general relativity.
6. Euclid (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F02k02&q=Euclid)	c. 300 BC	Known as the "Father of Geometry"; authored The Elements, the most influential textbook in mathematical history.
7. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0372p&q=Gottfried+Wilhelm+Leibniz)	1646–1716	Co-inventor of infinitesimal calculus (developing standard notation like \(\int \) and d) and pioneer of the binary system.
8. Évariste Galois (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F02p0b&q=%C3%89variste+Galois)	1811–1832	Founded Galois theory and group theory, revolutionizing the study of algebraic equations before dying young at age 20.
9. David Hilbert (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F029rk&q=David+Hilbert)	1862–1943	Created Hilbert space, formalized mathematical logic, and proposed his famous list of 23 unsolved problems in 1900.
10. John von Neumann (https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F0421f&q=John+von+Neumann)	1903–1957	Made foundational contributions to game theory, quantum mechanics, set theory, and the architecture of modern digital computers.
