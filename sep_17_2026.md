## How the "Snatch" Game Works
The practice digitizes the ancient tradition of elders handing out physical red paper envelopes stuffed with cash (hongbao) to pass on good luck and blessings. On WeChat, it becomes a competitive game of speed: [3, 5, 6] 

   1. The Post: A user drops a digital red packet into a family, friend, or coworker group chat. [5] 
   2. The Settings: The sender loads the packet with a set amount of money (e.g., 100 Yuan) and specifies how many people can claim a piece of it (e.g., 5 people). [1] 
   3. The "Lucky Draw" Twist: Crucially, the sender usually chooses the randomized lottery option. The app's algorithm randomly splits the total pool into completely unequal amounts. One person might get 60 Yuan, while another gets 2 cents. [5, 7, 8, 9] 
   4. The Snatch: The moment the packet icon appears in the chat, group members must tap it as fast as possible. Since the number of packets is limited, the "fastest fingers" win the cash, while the slow ones are left empty-handed. [1, 5, 9]

## The Infrastructure: How Servers Survive a 1-Second Rush
From the platform's perspective (like Taobao, Shopee, or Amazon), a 1-second snatch creates a massive spike in traffic that can crash standard databases. To survive thousands of orders hitting at the exact same second, engineers use an architecture called Inventory Pre-warming:

[User Traffic Spike] ──> [ Redis Cache (Instant Check) ] ──> [ Success Queue ] ──> [ Main Database ]
                                │
                        (If Inventory = 0)
                                │
                                └──> [ "Sold Out" Page ]

Instead of letting every user check the main database to see if an item is available (which slows down the system), the server "pre-warms" the data by moving the inventory count into a ultra-fast, temporary memory cache (like Redis).

* 
* The cache acts like a digital gatekeeper. It quickly counts down from 100 to 0 in a few milliseconds.
* The first 100 requests pass through to the success queue to pay.
* The remaining thousands of requests are instantly rejected and sent to a "Sold Out" screen, protecting the app from crashing.

## The Anatomy of the 11.11 Flash Sale
To survive and win during the midnight rush, you have to navigate a highly strategic, multi-week ecosystem:

* 
* The Pre-Sale Period (Late October): Long before November 11, merchants open up "deposit locking." Shoppers pay a tiny fraction of the price (e.g., $2) to secure the flash-sale price. On 11.11, they pay the remaining balance.
* The Midnight Rush (00:00:00 on Nov 11): This is when the true "snatching" happens. Billions of dollars in transactions occur in the first few minutes. If your payment isn't processed within the first 2–3 seconds, the item is gone.
* Stackable Red Packets (Hongbao): Leading up to the sale, platforms force users to play in-app mini-games to collect digital red packets. These packets, combined with platform-wide coupons (e.g., "Save $5 for every $50 spent"), can be stacked on top of flash-sale discounts.
