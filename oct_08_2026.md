# Periodic Table of Elements
The Periodic Table of Elements is an ordered, tabular arrangement of all 118 discovered chemical elements, organized by their atomic number, electron configurations, and recurring chemical properties.

The table was famously introduced by Dmitri Mendeleev in 1869. It is split into horizontal rows called periods and vertical columns called groups or families, which cluster elements with similar behaviors together.

The abbreviation HG refers to the chemical element mercury, which occupies a unique spot on the periodic table of elements [8]. As you can see from this image, the periodic table organizes all discovered chemical elements into rows and columns [3]. Looking at the lower-middle section under column twelve, you can spot the symbol Hg, which represents mercury with the atomic number eighty [8]. This chart helps us see that mercury is a transition metal, grouped alongside other metals like copper, silver, and gold. However, mercury stands out because it is the only metal that remains a liquid at standard room temperature. Knowing how to read this table helps scientists unlock the fundamental building blocks of our entire universe [19].

## Key Regions of the Periodic Table


* Alkali & Alkaline Earth Metals (Groups 1 & 2): Highly reactive, shiny metals. Elements like Sodium (Na) and Calcium (Ca) belong here.
* Transition Metals (Groups 3–12): Hard, dense metals that conduct electricity well. This massive block includes Iron (Fe), Gold (Au), Copper (Cu), and Mercury (Hg).
* Metalloids: Elements touching the jagged "stair-step" line (like Silicon and Boron) that share properties of both metals and nonmetals.
* Halogens (Group 17): Extremely reactive nonmetals like Fluorine (F) and Chlorine (Cl).
* Noble Gases (Group 18): Odorless, colorless, and completely unreactive (inert) gases like Helium (He) and Neon (Ne).
