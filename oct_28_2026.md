# short articles
The event you are referring to is a major incident that occurred at GEEKCON 2026 (the cybersecurity conference formerly known as GeekPwn). 

On October 24, 2026, during a live "physical cybersecurity" demonstration at the West Bund Art Center in Shanghai, a security contestant successfully hacked a mainstream automatic drone/quadcopter plane on stage.

Key Details of the Incident:

* The Setup: Judges had locked the drone inside a "geofence" (a software-enforced no-fly zone) using GPS spoofing, completely blocking its official controller from forcing a takeoff.
* The Hack: Without physically touching the device, a contestant exploited an undisclosed vulnerability to override the system, bypass the no-fly restriction, and seize total control of the automated aircraft. 
* The Aftermath: Right after the audience cheered, someone called the police. Local law enforcement arrived at the perimeter, interrupted the conference, and took the contestants and judges away to assist with a sudden investigation. The rest of that afternoon's anti-drone demonstration was entirely canceled.(Note: "110" refers to the emergency telephone number for the police in mainland China, which was dialed by an attendee or observer during the live demonstration).



During the GEEKCON 2026 event, the organizer (DarkNavy) and the researchers abided by their standard "Pwn2Own-style" competition rules and did not openly name the vendor or display the manufacturer's brand in plaintext on stage. 

However, tech industry disclosures and cybersecurity community updates heavily point to the involved manufacturer:
* The Manufacturer's Identity: Reports and insider statements confirm that the drone targeted was a "mainstream consumer drone" from a "domestic leading drone enterprise" . In the global and Chinese consumer drone market, DJI  is the dominant manufacturer that fits this description. 
* The Escalation: The situation escalated because an on-site representative from that specific drone vendor was the one who placed the 110 call to the Shanghai police, accusing the event of "instigating others to hack drones" after seeing their product's security restriction remotely bypassed.
  
The decision to involve law enforcement drew heavy criticism from prominent Chinese cybersecurity experts and GEEKCON founder Wang Qi, who noted that weaponizing the police against white-hat vulnerability disclosures damages the collaborative security ecosystem.
